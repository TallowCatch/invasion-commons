# Literature Positioning Matrix

## Short position

This work combines sequential commons, explicit actor-overseer capability gaps, local/global safety predicates, oversight architecture comparison, and a controlled LLM strategy bridge. The contribution should be described as a combination and benchmark formulation, not as a claim that any single ingredient is entirely new.

## Cluster notes

### 1. Sequential social dilemmas / MARL benchmarks

Existing work studies repeated interaction where individually beneficial behavior can harm collective outcomes. This supports the Harvest substrate and the use of resource health, welfare, and failure metrics. Current references that support this positioning are Leibo et al. (2017), Melting Pot 2.0, SocialJax, and the multi-agent cooperation review already cited in the paper.

What it does not cover directly is the actor-overseer capability gap. The present paper should say that it extends this benchmark family with an oversight layer and local/global safety diagnostic.

Missing references to verify: original Melting Pot 1.0 if the paper wants the benchmark lineage to include both Melting Pot and Melting Pot 2.0.

### 2. Melting Pot / SocialJax-style environments

Existing work provides benchmark suites and baseline comparisons for multi-agent learning in social dilemmas. These works help justify using controlled environments rather than field data.

The present paper should avoid saying that Harvest Commons is novel as a social dilemma substrate. The novel part is the oversight/capability-gap layer and the LPGF diagnostic.

Current references: Agapiou et al. (2022) and Guo et al. (2025).

Missing references to verify: Melting Pot 1.0 and any relevant held-out generalization benchmark source if the paper discusses benchmark methodology more broadly.

### 3. Scalable oversight benchmarks

Existing scalable-oversight work studies how supervision changes when the actor or task becomes more capable than the overseer can directly verify. Current references already include Bowman et al. (2022) and Engels et al. (2025). A verified additional source is Sudhir, Kaunismaa, and Panickssery (2025), "A Benchmark for Scalable Oversight Protocols," which frames scalable oversight around comparing oversight protocols.

What this paper adds is an interacting multi-agent resource setting. The failure is not only that a single answer is hard to verify. The failure can arise because many locally acceptable actions combine into unsafe aggregate dynamics.

Missing references to verify: the HDO multi-agent oversight paper mentioned in meeting notes. A quick search did not identify a reliable exact citation, so it should remain a TODO until title/authors/arXiv link are confirmed.

### 4. Multi-agent governance / institutional design

Ostrom supports the institutional framing around monitoring, rules, enforcement, local participation, and cost. This paper operationalizes those ideas as local oversight, global signal, hybrid oversight, detection recall, delay, targeting capacity, and intervention cost.

What existing governance work does not necessarily provide is a scalable-oversight capability-gap benchmark. The paper should use governance literature to support the mechanism language, not to claim field-policy calibration.

Current reference: Ostrom (1990).

Missing references to verify: "Governing multi-agent systems" and "Incentivising Monitoring in Open Normative Systems." These should not be cited until exact bibliographic details are checked.

### 5. LLM-generated social-dilemma strategies

Willis, Du, and Leibo motivate the idea of strategies as artifacts generated by language models and evaluated in social dilemmas. This supports the LLM bridge in the paper.

The present paper differs because it uses generated strategies to test oversight architectures rather than to make the LLM population itself the main scientific object. The bridge should remain described as offline structured strategy generation.

Current references: Willis et al. (2025) and Willis et al. (2026).

Missing references to verify: Concordia or related simulated-agent frameworks if the paper later expands into richer agent simulations.

### 6. Live LLM-agent safety benchmarks

Live-agent safety benchmarks are relevant for future work but not for the current paper. They would matter if the project moves to online LLM action selection, tool use, memory, and task completion under safety constraints.

The current paper should not cite live-agent benchmarks as if it has run that kind of evaluation. They should appear only in future-work positioning unless the project actually builds a live-agent module.

Missing references to verify: Agent-SafetyBench, OpenAgentSafety, SafeArena, and any current live-agent evaluation frameworks that are directly relevant.

## Contribution statement to use

The safest contribution statement is:

"This paper is a scalable-oversight benchmark in a sequential commons. It combines a shared-resource social dilemma substrate with an explicit actor-overseer capability gap, local and global safety predicates, an oversight-architecture comparison, and a controlled bridge to model-generated structured strategies."

Avoid:

- "No one has studied this before."
- "Hybrid oversight solves scalable oversight."
- "This is a live LLM-agent benchmark."
- "The scenarios are policy-calibrated."
