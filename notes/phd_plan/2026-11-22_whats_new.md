# What's new, compared with existing work (gap 5, 22 Nov 2026)

**One paragraph for a paper.** Earlier work covers four pieces:
- **Deterrence by imperfect checking:** a fine times the chance of being caught must exceed the gain from breaking the rule (Becker-style deterrence; inspection games).
- **A single learner that only sometimes sees its reward:** Monitored MDPs (Parisi et al. 2024).
- **Random audits of one agent that may hide evidence:** Gans & Holden 2026; Blindfold (Zhu et al. 2026).
- **Protocols for one untrusted model with a limited audit budget:** AI Control (Greenblatt et al. 2024). Per-agent monitors against coordinated LLM attackers: Makins et al. 2026.

In multi-agent RL, interveners in social dilemmas see every agent:
- planners and incentive designers (Baumann et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2020; IQ-Flow; the AI Economist; Large Legislative Models);
- the one budgeted-audit study (Casheekar et al. 2026) is a one-step game whose agents cannot evade the audits;
- decentralised control (Du et al. 2022; Ma et al. 2024) has no overseer at all.

We study overseers that check only a few agents at a time, possibly split across regions that each see only local information, in sequential social dilemmas whose agents learn. This gives three kinds of result the earlier work does not:
1. **A measurable locality number that predicts when an oversight budget can be split across regions** (prediction; being measured).
2. **Learning effects that deterrence theory does not predict:**
   - with an imperfect overseer, one big check per step does clearly worse than seven small ones at the same average penalty (342 vs 479 apples);
   - repairing agents that already misbehave depends on how often the overseer checks and whom it targets (149 to 325 apples for 1 to 7 random checks; 348 for 2 checks on repeat offenders);
   - removing the overseer undoes everything (18 of 18 runs fall back to 68 apples).
3. **A capability ladder for LLM inspectors** (planned), in which each model size's misses, false alarms and token cost turn into whether learning agents are deterred.

## In more detail

| Strand | What it has | What it lacks, for us |
|---|---|---|
| Deterrence economics, inspection games | the fine × chance ≥ gain rule; an inspector with limited inspections | learning agents, many agents in a shared dilemma, space |
| Monitored MDPs (Parisi et al. 2024) | a general framework for rewards seen only when monitored, with monitoring costs | one agent, no other agents, no one trying to avoid being seen |
| Gans & Holden 2026; Blindfold | when random audits work against an agent that can hide evidence or predict audits | one agent; theory or LLM certification, not learning populations |
| AI Control (Greenblatt et al. 2024) | an audit budget, safety vs usefulness, a red team | one untrusted model, no social dilemma |
| Multi-agent AI Control (Makins et al. 2026) | many LLM agents splitting an attack against per-agent monitors | no learning, no dilemma, monitors not budgeted or targeted |
| MARL interveners (Baumann; LIO; IQ-Flow; AI Economist; LLM) | learning agents in dilemmas, an outside party shaping rewards | the intervener sees everyone |
| Casheekar et al. 2026 | random audits with a budget, learning agents, where to put the checks | one-step game, agents cannot see or dodge audits |
| Du et al. 2022; Ma et al. 2024 | decentralised agents with local information, a locality condition (ξ), many testbeds | no overseer, cooperative agents |

## What we found that deterrence theory alone would not predict

**Measured (Harvest, IPPO, 3 seeds each):**

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| With mistakes, how checks are spread matters even at the same average penalty | k = 1 vs 7: 342 vs 479 (misses), 382 vs 482 and 470 vs 509 (false alarms) |
| Repair of existing over-harvesting depends on check frequency and targeting | 1/2/4/7 random checks: 149/197/274/325 apples; 2 checks on repeat offenders: 348 |
| Compliance, not conversion | without the overseer, 18 of 18 runs fall back to 68 apples |
| Targeting is only as good as its signal | checking by location catches 5% of over-harvests vs 28% for random |

**Still predictions or bot-level only:**
- the locality number predicting the decentralisation gap;
- migration by learning agents;
- the LLM capability ladder.

## What we should not claim

- **That the deterrence threshold itself is new.** It is Becker's rule, here confirmed for learning agents and in space.
- **That the overseer's design problem is solved.** Settings are searched on a grid.
- **That our derived catching formulas are deep.** They are simple probability, and their value is in being testable.
