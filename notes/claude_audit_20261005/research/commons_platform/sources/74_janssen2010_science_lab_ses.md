# Lab Experiments for the Study of Social-Ecological Systems

- **Authors:** Marco A. Janssen, Robert Holahan, Allen Lee, Elinor Ostrom
- **Year:** 2010
- **Venue / identifier:** Science 328(5978): 613–617; DOI 10.1126/science.1183532
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1183532
- **Source type:** academic
- **Date accessed:** 14 Oct 2026
- **Depth:** ABSTRACT ONLY (Crossref/OpenAlex records, plus the EurekAlert press release of 29 Apr 2010). Paywalled, supplementary material not reached. Grid, regrowth and treatment details come from Janssen & Rollins 2012 (75), which describes the same experimental environment and data, and from Janssen 2010 E&S (76).

## Verbatim quotes

1. Abstract:
   > "We introduce an experimental environment that involves spatial and temporal resource dynamics in order to capture these two critical variables identified in field research."
2. > "Previous behavioral experiments of commons dilemmas have found that people are willing to engage in costly punishment, frequently generating increases in gross benefits, contrary to game-theoretical predictions based on a static pay-off function."
3. > "Results in our experimental environment find that costly punishment is again used but lacks a gross positive effect on resource harvesting unless combined with communication."
4. > "These findings illustrate the importance of careful generalization from the laboratory to the world of policy."
5. Press release (EurekAlert 505494): treatments described as "costly fines, communication, a combination of both and a period where neither punishment nor communication is allowed"; Ostrom quoted: "When participants make decisions without communicating, they overharvest."

## Relevance
This is the human ancestor of Commons Harvest: Melting Pot's `commons_harvest__open.py` docstring says the density-dependent regrowth "was first described in Janssen et al (2010) and adapted for multi-agent reinforcement learning in Perolat et al (2017)" (see 01), and Perolat 2017 cites it as the "dynamic game used in human behavioral experiments" (09). Its central result — sanctions alone do not help in a spatial, real-time commons; sanctions plus communication do — is a direct prior for any fine-based overseer in a 2D game.
