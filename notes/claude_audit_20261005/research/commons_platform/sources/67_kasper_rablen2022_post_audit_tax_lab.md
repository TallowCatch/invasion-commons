## Verbatim quotes

1. The canonical lab "tax evasion game" (TEG) audit setting:
   > "The control treatment is the canonical TEG, where the random audit probability is constant at 0.1 and announced to participants."
2. How the tax and fine are chosen (calibrated to a review, not derived):
   > "The tax system parameters are calibrated according to the considerations detailed in Alm (2019). Specifically, in all treatments, reported income is taxed at a rate of 25 percent and the fine for noncompliance is twice the evaded tax."
3. Theory benchmark:
   > "the seminal Allingham-Sandmo (AS) anal- ysis of tax compliance, Allingham and Sandmo (1972), predicts a zero post-audit effect for audited taxpayers who do not receive a fine, and a positive post-audit effect for fined tax- payers."
4. Lab finding that contradicts it (the "bomb-crater" effect):
   > "This prediction of a positive average post-audit effect is broadly in line with a growing empirical literature on post-audit effects, but discordant with experimental findings, which are instead consistent with a (weakly) negative average post-audit effect."
5. Meta-analytic fact quoted from Alm & Malézieux (2021):
   > "or full-compliance account for some 65 percent of all reporting decisions in TEGs, ac- cording to the meta-analysis of Alm and Mal´ ezieux (2021)."
   (Sentence begins on the previous page; the preceding words refer to zero compliance.)
6. Design: three treatments, 85 / 89 / 99 participants, 25–30 reporting decisions each; an endogenous rule audits "the 10% of the participants who reported the lowest amounts of income".

## Relevance
Gives Ameer a concrete, cited lab default for random audits: probability 0.1, known to subjects, fine equal to twice the evaded tax, tax 25%. With p = 0.1 and a 2x fine the expected penalty is below the gain from evading, so a risk-neutral selfish agent should evade; humans mostly do not (over-compliance), and audits can lower compliance in the next round. These are precisely the departures from Allingham–Sandmo that an LLM-agent audit study should test for, and the random-versus-targeted (endogenous) audit rule is a ready-made treatment.
