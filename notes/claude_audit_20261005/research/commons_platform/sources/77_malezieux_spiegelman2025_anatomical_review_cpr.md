## Verbatim quotes

1. Group sizes used in human CPR experiments:
   > "It bears mention that the group sizes in experimental protocols show a relatively restricted range, nearly always in the single digits; the harmful effects of large groups may depend on more extreme definitions of 'larger.'"
2. Doubling regrowth has a human-lab precedent:
   > "in Hine and Gifford ( Reference Hine and Gifford 1996 ), the average regeneration rate was 2 (i.e., a doubling of the fish remaining in the pool), but in the unknown treatment it varied between 1.25 to 2.50."
3. Growth rate sets the difficulty of the dilemma:
   > "In this case, a higher replenishment rate corresponds to an 'easier' social dilemma."
4. Audit probabilities used for quota enforcement in CPR experiments:
   > "Two (2/6) papers found that a quota with weak enforcement (generally a 10 or 20% chance of being audited along with a rather low fine if caught extracting above the quota) leads to a decrease in individual extraction compared to the open-access regime (Cardenas, Reference Cardenas 2004 ; Velez & Lopez, Reference Velez and Lopez 2013 ), however, four (4/6) others found that the benefit of such a quota is not sustainable"
   > "with penalties for harvesting more than one's own quota imposed with zero percent (social norms), 10% (mild law, non-incentive compatible) or 50% (theoretically deterrent) probabilities." (Leibbrandt & Lynham 2018)
5. Peer fines rarely pay off:
   > "only 14% (1/7) of the studies finds substantial efficiency gains from allowing players to 'fine' each other for overextraction."
6. How to set a baseline:
   > "Second, the present insights must be used to calibrate a baseline game with rather low cooperation, so that new effects are rather easy to detect."

## Relevance
The best single source on how human CPR games are parameterised: groups are almost always under 10, regeneration rates in dynamic games range roughly 1.05–2.5 (doubling is used), and quota enforcement uses audit chances of about 10–20% (weak), with 50% as the "theoretically deterrent" level and 0% as a norms-only control. Its methodological advice is to calibrate the baseline so cooperation is low, leaving room to detect an institution's effect. These are cited ranges Ameer can place his Fishery/Forest/River and audit settings against.
