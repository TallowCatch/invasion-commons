## Relevance
The origin of the Schelling diagram: payoff to choosing L or R plotted against the number of others choosing R, for symmetric N-person binary choices. Perolat 2017 adopted it because Leibo's 2-player empirical payoff matrix does not extend to N > 2 (09), and Hughes 2018 turned it into the formal N-player sequential-social-dilemma test (10). The helmet example is also the cleanest statement of why an external rule (an overseer) can make everyone better off: "If the league made us do it ... nobody would mind."
