## 1. The short answer

1. **Your settings are no less grounded than the field's, but your validation is.**
   - Every commons platform hand-sets its numbers. Melting Pot's regrowth rates change from paper to paper, and its shipped code differs from both papers [01, 09, 10]. GovSim's settings are only "inspired by" Gordon and Ostrom [60]. Willis et al. chose the stock size "to keep numerical values convenient" [41].
   - What the field *does* do, and you don't yet, is **prove each game is a social dilemma at the chosen settings**, with a Schelling diagram or an equilibrium calculation [09, 10, 20, 62, 71]. It also evaluates against **mixed populations** (focal and background) [05, 07, 08].
   - So the fix is to follow the field's procedure, not to copy its numbers.
2. **Don't build a new 2D engine for novelty.**
   - The engine is not where the novelty is. LLMs in SocialJax Harvest were published on 9 October [OpenSocialJax, 22]. An LLM setting taxes for RL agents in Commons Harvest was published in 2024 [27, Large Legislative Models; checked by hand].
   - What nobody has done: **an overseer that audits and fines LLM agents in a spatial commons, evaluated the way AI control evaluates monitors.** That means a fixed audit budget, a red team, and safety and usefulness measured separately [80–84].
   - So **build the overseer yourself**, as a layer that works on any game, and **attach it to standard games**: SocialJax/OpenSocialJax Commons Harvest for space, Willis's code for scale, and your own text testbed for clean measurement.
   - Build your own grid only as a faithful copy of the SocialJax Harvest rules, and only if the OpenSocialJax hooks or costs fail an admission test.
3. **The study list** (section 6) has 39 studies in five phases. Phase 0 makes the current paper defensible at no LLM cost. The rest gives three or four papers.

## 4. Richard Willis's work (`findings/willis_rundown.md`)

**The code** (`willis-richard/emergent_llm`, commit `be78ff9`, MIT licence) [40]:
1. Each LLM writes a strategy twice: first in words, then as a Python class.
   - The class sees its own actions and payoffs, an anonymous count of cooperating opponents per round, and the stock.
   - Actions are C or D only.
2. The Common Pool Resource game is about 30 lines.
   - C takes S/(2n) and D takes S/n.
   - The stock regrows logistically (rate 2, capacity k = 4n) over 20 rounds.
   - There is no enforcer.
3. **Scale:** 9,216 strategies, from three models × eight attitude words. Code passes an AST whitelist check.
4. **Three analyses:**
   - behavioural fingerprints (5,461 dimensions, with PCA);
   - self-play over every Collective/Selfish mixture at n = 4–256;
   - Fermi imitation over (model, attitude) among 512 agents.
5. **Finding:** welfare efficiency in the commons is 91% at n = 4 but 15% at n = 64.

**His other work** [42–46]:
- the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma with strategies as code (AAMAS 2025);
- minimal reward transfer (JAAMAS 2024), whose institution is a contract, explicitly preferred to punishment;
- self-interest levels in Melting Pot (IJCAI 2025), which names "a sanctioning mechanism" as future work;
- co-authorship of SocialJax.

**Gaps you can fill.** No Willis paper tests an external overseer. The 2026 paper calls for "cooperation-supporting institutions" and leaves them untested [41].

**Adding audits and fines** [40]:
- With the existing strategies unaware of audits: about 150–250 lines and no LLM cost.
- With strategies regenerated to know the rules: about 300–450 lines, then Ollama calls.

**Becker threshold in his game:** F\* = S_t/(2nq), so 2/q at full stock, for any group size. Allowing for the defector's own future loss, the net gain is 1.30 at n = 4 and 1.97 at n = 64.

**Traps:**
- A defection in the last round *raises* welfare (+2.0), so an overseer must not be scored as failing for allowing it.
- Paper and code differ on the number of generations (2,000 against 1,000).
- No random seeds are set.

## 5. Decision: build on an existing platform, or build my own?

| Criterion | Own 2D engine | SocialJax (RL agents) | OpenSocialJax (LLM agents) | Melting Pot / Concordia | Willis `emergent_llm` | Your text testbed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Novelty of the *engine* | Low: reviewers ask "why not Melting Pot or SocialJax?" | — | — | — | — | — |
| Novelty of *an overseer on it* | Same as the others | High (none exists) [20, 21] | **Highest**: LLM agents, no overseer yet [22] | High; Concordia's fishery "penalties" exist only in prompt text [14] | High; institutions are their stated future work [41] | Already shown, in part |
| Comparability with the literature | None | High | High (published days ago) | Highest, but heavy | High for scale | Low |
| Cost per seed | You choose | CPU or GPU only | $2–4 with one call per step (estimate from $0.00025 per call; its README says about $4 per Clean Up seed with a cheap model [23]) | Lab2D needs RL; Concordia about 4 calls per action (estimate) [13] | About $0 with existing strategies | $0.04–0.06 |
| Effort to add an overseer | Native | Low to moderate: a wrapper with its own state; allowances must enter observations [20] | Moderate: a score-changing hook plus an overseer prompt block; switch off hidden rules [23] | High for Lab2D; moderate for a Concordia game master | 150–450 lines [40] | Done |
| Clean deterrence threshold | Possible | As a band | As a band | As a band | **Yes**, with binary actions | **Yes** |
| Connection to your group | — | Yali and Richard co-authors | Yali co-author | — | Richard | — |

**Recommendation (H3 supported, with one refinement): build the overseer layer yourself, and run it on standard games.** The pieces:

1. **An oversight layer that works on any game.**
   - Every K steps it reads a per-agent ledger. It sets allowances, audits under a fixed budget, fines, remembers, and optionally reads messages.
   - It can be a rule, a small LLM ("trusted") or a large LLM ("untrusted").
   - This is your contribution and your code.
2. **Your text testbed** stays as the measurement lab, where thresholds can be predicted exactly.
3. **Willis's game** for scale (n up to 256) and cultural evolution, together with Richard.
4. **SocialJax Commons Harvest with RL agents**, for cheap sweeps and for red teams trained against the overseer (the two-level training of the AI Economist [25]).
5. **OpenSocialJax with LLM agents**, as the spatial headline.
   - Cut the cost with high-level actions every K steps. That is about 250 calls per game, close to your current games.
   - Report LLM agents as the **focal** population against RL **background** populations, as Melting Pot does.
6. **Fallback:** if the OpenSocialJax hooks or costs fail an admission test, write a small Python grid that copies the SocialJax Harvest rules exactly. Cite it as a port, not a new game.

**Why this is the most novel option, not just the safest.** A new grid adds nothing reviewers count as new. An overseer evaluated across standard games, with a fixed budget, a red team, separate safety and usefulness, a capability gap and an LLM overseer, does not exist [80–84, 92; adversarial searches below]. It is also evaluable "as the literature intends", because each piece reuses that literature's protocol.

**Hypothesis H4 is supported.** One LLM call per step costs about $1–16 per run in every published system: $0.78–15.63 [16], about $10 [Hypothetical Minds], about $4 [22]. Published systems that are affordable use high-level actions [16; Hypothetical Minds, see `spatial_game_options.md`].

5. Every result states its audit budget as a % of agent-rounds.
6. Every result says whether it measures **propensity** (normally prompted agents) or **worst case** (red team).
7. Report a suite of metrics: efficiency net of cost, equality, survival, over-usage, how many cheat and how much [71, 09, 60, 69].
8. Report cost in tokens and dollars, and release seeds and configs [60, 61].

## 7. Adversarial pass

1. **Could building my own engine be more novel?** Only if the paper were an environment paper. The evidence:
   - environment papers in this area come from large groups and become standard by adoption (Melting Pot, SocialJax, OpenSocialJax) [01, 20, 22];
   - a solo grid would be compared with them and asked why.

   *Kept as a fallback, as a faithful port.*
2. **Is the novelty already gone?**
   - Searched by hand: "Large Legislative Models" (arXiv:2410.08345) uses an LLM principal that sets **taxes** on **RL** agents in Commons Harvest and Clean Up. It has no audits, detection or fines, and no LLM agents.
   - A 2026 search for LLM overseers that audit or fine LLM agents in grid commons found only task-pipeline auditing (for example BRA-Audit, arXiv:2608.14668), with no commons and no deterrence.
   - OpenSocialJax has no overseer [22].
   - Bell and Madisetti (ICML 2026) use a voted restitution rule in a text fishery [64].

   *The gap holds as of 14 October 2026. Re-check before each submission (see `refresh_targets.md`).*
3. **Does control-style evaluation fit a commons?**
   - Control assumes a worst-case adversary; commons work measures propensity [80, 99]. Reporting both is a strength only if each number is labelled.
   - Risk: one research cluster dominates the control literature [80–85]. The peer-reviewed anchors are [80] (ICML 2024), [83] (ICLR 2025) and [92] (NeurIPS 2025).
4. **Is "everyone hand-tunes" an excuse?** No. It moves the burden to validation (Schelling diagrams, sweeps, stated ranges). That is why Phase 0 comes first.
5. **Platform risk.**
   - OpenSocialJax is five days old. Pin its commit (`b5f59a3`) and expect changes.
   - SocialJax pitfalls: the default shared reward, 7× individual reward, and an int8 inventory counter that may overflow [20].
6. **Human contrast as a result.** In humans, sanctions without talk fail [71, 74]. Your LLMs were deterred by fines alone (L3/L4). I1 tests this directly, and it may be a headline.

**Insufficient evidence** (fewer than three independent sources, or abstract only):
- the details of Bell and Madisetti [64];
- the range of audit rates across tax experiments [68, 69];
- Concordia's cost per step (estimate) [13];
- the venues of [94] and [95].

## 8. Hypotheses: verdicts

| | Verdict |
