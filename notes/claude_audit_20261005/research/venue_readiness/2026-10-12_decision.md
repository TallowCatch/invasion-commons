## 1. Bottom line

- **The paper as it stands fits a workshop.** With text fixes, it can go to an AAAI-27 workshop (20 Nov) plus arXiv.
- **What a main-track paper (IJCAI, ICML or NeurIPS 2027) needs.** Most of the extra work is LLM evidence:
  - (i) the identification test: the same expected fine at different audit rates;
  - (ii) held-out populations and pre-specified prompt paraphrases;
  - (iii) more model families, including at least one closed frontier model, so that more than two models over-take on purpose.
- **What the programmed-agent half needs.** Positioning, not more experiments.
- **Compute.** It is not the constraint. On Ollama's current credit pricing, the core follow-up on Ollama costs about $10. Closed frontier models are not on Ollama and would cost roughly $20–60 per 100 games through their own APIs or OpenRouter.

**H1** (main track expects ≥4–6 LLMs, including a closed frontier model):
- The closed-model part is **confirmed**.
- "≥4–6" as a hard minimum is **refuted**: AI Control used 2 models.
- The real weakness is that only 2 models over-take on purpose.

**H2** (reviewers will ask for the identification test): **plausible, not documented** in any public review. Current practice (27–29) makes it likely at a safety or agents venue.

## 4. Threats to novelty (sources 55–69, adversarial pass)

- **Not cited and must be.**
  - Bracale Syrnikov et al. 2026, "Institutional AI" (2601.11369) (55): recorded, escalating sanctions on LLM agents cut collusion from 50% to 5.6%, a prompt-only prohibition does nothing, and they write the Becker condition pS ≥ Δπ.
  - Konuganti & Co 2026 (29).
  - RepuNet (63), CARP (69), SODE (58), Burnat & Davidson (60), Agarwal et al. (62), PIMMUR (65), construct validity (66), evaluation awareness (67).
- **Apparent conflict with Okamoto et al.** (AIES 2026; 57). Under imperative or informational wording, gpt-oss-120b is "rule-anchored", and Nemotron complies less once a fine is named.
  - **Defence:** Okamoto's *discretionary* wording ("you can still use any vendor") is the analogue of our explicit wording. There, GPT-OSS-120B compliance runs 0/74/100/100 and Nemotron's 25/50/83/100 across no, small, medium and large fines (our novelty notes, Table 10). That is the same pattern we find.
  - **Text needed:** model behaviour depends on the task and the wording, not on a fixed trait of the model.
- **"Myopic":** already used by Bracale (59) and SODE (58). Give it an operational definition, or drop the word.
- **Null results.** No paper varies the stated audit probability and the fine separately for LLM agents. No paper uses a reviewer that sets allowances in an LLM commons. None tests rank-based audit targeting with LLMs. None states a one-round against whole-game threshold. So the core contribution stands.
- **Scooping watch:**
  - penseralabs/becker-agents is still 3 commits from 22 Sep, with no results, and its harness varies audit percentage separately from penalty (56). Check before every submission.
  - Konuganti & Co show the audit-probability sweep is publishable. Whoever publishes the matched test first takes the open question.

## 6. Gaps, ranked

**Required for a main track**
1. **L4, the identification and transfer test** (frozen protocol, about 200 games, about $10). On 10 new populations:
   - calibrate with no fine, then freeze the prediction;
   - E24 and E30 replicate the threshold out of sample;
   - q = 1/3 at F = 12 and 15, and q = 1/12 at F = 48 and 60, hold the expected fine fixed;
   - P0, P24 and P30 test transfer across wordings.
2. **More families.** Run a 10-game no-fine pilot on 3–5 new families: two or three closed (Claude, GPT and Gemini, cheap tier first) and two open (DeepSeek, GLM or Kimi). Run the full threshold test for every family that over-takes on purpose. Families that never over-take are reported as a result (as with Mistral).
3. **Related work and framing.** Cite and contrast the uncited papers in §4. Address the conflict with Okamoto. Define "myopic" operationally or drop it. Add a construct-validity paragraph.
4. **Statistics.** State the minimum detectable effect for 10 populations; apply a Holm correction across the fine grid; add the split-half and wording checks (already computed).

**Recommended**
5. An LLM version of Forest, to give a second game. It also answers the supervisor's network question about spillover on the ring.
6. A true no-audit, no-fine control (one cell per model).
7. Release the code and data at submission (this also anonymises the protocols).

**Optional**
8. A post hoc count of reasoning traces that mention being tested.
9. Detection delay for the audit-targeting comparison.
10. A link to human deterrence data.

## 8. Adversarial pass (self-critique)

1. **"Unfreezing invites endless loops."** Bound it: one frozen L4 protocol and one model-family extension, with no further additions before January. If L4 slips, go to NeurIPS in May rather than adding more.
2. **"Closed models may never over-take."** This is likely for some, as with Mistral. Run the no-fine pilot first, at about $1–6 per model. A "whether models over-take" result is still publishable.
3. **"TMLR, not a main track, may be the better home."** TMLR judges whether the claims are supported, not novelty, which suits a candid, heavily controlled paper. But the workshop already gives early visibility, and TMLR's no-overlap rule only excludes *archival* prior publication.
4. **"The confidence in the estimated deadlines is low."** The 2027 calls for IJCAI and ICML are not out. Re-check in November.
5. **"The pricing numbers came through a summarising fetch tool."** Confirm the plan type and credit balance on the dashboard before running anything.
